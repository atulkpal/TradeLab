# Lecture 3.3.2: Current vs. Non-Current

**Course:** 3 | **Chapter:** Chapter 3.3: The Balance Sheet | **Lecture:** 3.3.2

**Concept:** The balance sheet shows assets, liabilities, and shareholder equity.

---

Assets and liabilities are further classified as current and non-current. Current assets (like cash and inventory) are expected to be converted to cash within one year. Current liabilities (like payables and short-term debt) are due within one year. Non-current assets are long-term (like property and machinery). A healthy company typically has more current assets than current liabilities, indicating good liquidity. For example, a company like TCS typically has strong current assets due to its high cash reserves.
