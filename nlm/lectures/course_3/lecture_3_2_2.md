# Lecture 3.2.2: Accrual vs. Cash Basis

**Course:** 3 | **Chapter:** Chapter 3.2: The Three Financial Statements | **Lecture:** 3.2.2

**Concept:** The balance sheet, income statement, and cash flow statement.

---

A crucial concept is the difference between accrual accounting and cash accounting. The Income Statement is prepared on an accrual basis, meaning revenue is recognized when earned, not when cash is received. This can create discrepancies. The Cash Flow Statement reconciles this difference by showing how much actual cash the company generated. A profitable company on the income statement can still have negative cash flow, which is a major red flag. For example, a company might show high sales but have unpaid receivables.
