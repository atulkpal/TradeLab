# Lecture 3.7.1: What is the P/E Ratio?

**Course:** 3 | **Chapter:** Chapter 3.7: The P/E Ratio | **Lecture:** 3.7.1

**Concept:** The P/E ratio compares the stock price to its earnings.

---

The Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio is the most popular valuation metric. It is calculated as the company's current stock price divided by its earnings per share (EPS). It tells you how much investors are willing to pay for one rupee of earnings. A high P/E suggests investors expect higher future growth, while a low P/E might suggest the stock is undervalued or that the company is facing challenges. It's a crucial tool for comparing companies in the same sector. For example, if Infosys has a P/E of 25 and TCS has a P/E of 28, investors are paying more for TCS's earnings.
