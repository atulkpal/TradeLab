# Lecture 1.2.2: The IPO Process in India

**Course:** 1 | **Chapter:** Chapter 1.2: Why Companies Go Public & IPOs | **Lecture:** 1.2.2

**Concept:** An IPO is the first sale of stock to the public to raise capital.

---

The Indian IPO process follows a structured cycle. First, the company files a Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP) with SEBI, detailing its financials and risks. Once approved, it opens its issue to the public for a specific period, typically 3-5 days. Investors can bid for shares within the price band. At the end of the subscription period, the price is determined based on demand and shares are allotted. Finally, the company lists on the NSE and BSE. In India, IPO allotment often happens via a lottery system when oversubscribed, as seen with the LIC IPO.
